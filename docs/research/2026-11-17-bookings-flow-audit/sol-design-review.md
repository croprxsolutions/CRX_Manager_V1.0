The design does not yet give purchasing a reliable buy quantity or date. Its largest gaps are demand counted twice as bookings become orders or jobs, timing lost on those handoffs, and “Firm” holds that can be overbooked or taken by another order. These findings are based on the supplied packet; I did not inspect or run the app.

| # | Severity | What goes wrong in a concrete case | Recommended design change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | BLOCKER | `quote_product_draws` records quantity per **product**, not section. The same product is booked for March and August; a 60-gallon partial draw cannot be assigned reliably to either window. `_sync_planned_holds` currently consumes the earliest lines first. | Record each draw’s source quote item or section and remaining quantity by line. ### Simpler first version

Use a **single dated demand ledger and one purchasing screen**. Start with one timing per section, three confidence levels, and explicit “date required” and “past window” queues. Count Firm and Likely separately, promote scheduled jobs to committed demand, and let staff place POs manually. A transparent projected balance is useful before a precise “Order X by date” instruction; show that instruction only when use date, lead time, units, and incoming PO date are known. Defer D7 to a separate billing change unless its invoice correctness and idempotency are resolved in the same release.
