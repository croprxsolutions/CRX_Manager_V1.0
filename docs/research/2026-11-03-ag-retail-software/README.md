# Copy Agvance's money discipline, not its breadth

**Bottom line:** The best-documented ag retail system, Agvance by SSI, is built on one idea that CRX Manager already half-follows: every step of a sale is its own record with its own status, and money and inventory only move at specific, auditable events. AgWorks AgOS (the product Mason saw) is almost undocumented in public, so most of what we can learn about "how ag does software" comes from Agvance, and what we know about AgOS is limited to its module list and a few inferences. CRX already matches or beats the big suites on quote price-locking, landlord/tenant split invoicing, applicator jobs and traceability, and database-enforced money safety. Its real gaps are narrower than "no general ledger": prepay is not tied to specific product, the Order-page "Bill Split" card saves percentages that nothing reads, credit memos are applied by hand, restricted-use (RUP) checks only warn, there are no tie-out checks proving the books balance internally, and there is no unified work-order/ticket object that reserves product, loads, and bills as one thing. We recommend a three-phase plan: protect money and prove balances before the planned 2027-01-01 switch-over from the legacy system (Phase 1), then build the accounting hand-off and the prepay redesign (Phase 2), then add multi-location, lot-level stock, and a grower portal only when a business trigger appears (Phase 3). Several items reopen things Mason parked or decided; those are marked "Mason decision" and none is reversed silently. Mason's "Advantage" does not exist as a Cultura product (details below), and the AgWorks questions in section 6 are the fastest way to close the evidence gap.

## 0. One-page executive summary

**What was asked.** How do the leading ag retail software products organize modules, the end-to-end sale, accounting, inventory and screens, and what should CRX adopt, in what order?

**The naming issue, stated plainly.** The research found **no Cultura product called "Advantage."** Cultura Technologies is a Constellation Software company whose own site lists brands such as Greenstone, Agvision and others, but nothing named Advantage ([Cultura](https://culturatech.com)). The names nearest to what was said are three different things: **Agvance** (made by Software Solutions Integrated, "SSI", not Cultura), **AgVantage Software** (a separate vendor of ag retail and co-op software with an eAgvantage mobile product, [AgGateway profile](https://aggateway.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/APM/pages/69895044/AgVantage+Software)), and **Greenstone AGRIS** (Cultura's ag ERP, [SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/AGRIS)). No link between Agvance or AgVantage and Cultura was found. If a salesperson said "Advantage" about a Cultura product, ask which one they meant. This report treats Agvance as the main stand-in because it is the only one with a public help center.

**Evidence quality (read this first).**

| Product | Public evidence | What we can say |
|---|---|---|
| Agvance (SSI) | Large public help center | Document flow, statuses, prepay, splits, voids, finance charges, month-end: well supported |
| AgWorks AgOS | One marketing page, one brochure, app-store listings | Module list and field apps only. Sale flow, accounting and inventory rules: **not public** |
| Cultura (Greenstone AGRIS) and others | Directory listings, press, one prepay-interest doc title | Scope only; workflow detail essentially absent |

**The five things that matter most.**

1. **Separate the physical event from the billing event.** Agvance records that product was loaded on a ticket before anything is billed; loading moves inventory, invoicing moves money. CRX already has this split (delivery then invoice). Keep it, and add a "hold billing" flag and a single ticket object for blend and field work.
2. **Prepay is a customer balance with a purpose, never revenue.** Agvance forces prepay money onto a booking (product and price) or a typed generic prepay, and handles refunds as visible documents, not balance edits. CRX's prepay is generic credit only, and its product-linked version was shelved over a double-spend risk. Redesign it carefully (Mason decision).
3. **Void is for the open month; reverse is for closed months.** Agvance erases a current-month invoice but only offsets older ones. CRX's period close and "credit, don't cancel, posted invoices" rule already point this way.
4. **Close the month with equality checks.** Agvance's month-end insists that customer balances equal the A/R account, vendor balances equal A/P, and inventory value equals the asset account. CRX should add the same style of tie-out for what it does own (prepay balances, A/R, stock ledger), because it will feed QuickBooks Desktop.
5. **CRX should not become a general ledger.** Books stay in QuickBooks Desktop by Mason's decision. The right adoption is "emit clean, tie-out-able documents," not "build a GL."

**Recommended next step:** Mason sends the AgWorks questions in section 6 (they cost nothing), and Phase 1 starts with the three smallest money-safety items: remove or wire the dead Bill Split card, add prepay and A/R tie-out checks, and record refunds paid outside CRX.

## 1. How ag retail software works: the sale from booking to finance charge

Agricultural input retail has two quirks that ordinary invoicing software does not handle: **the sale is spread across months** (a grower commits and prepays in the fall for product that is delivered and applied in spring), and **the physical event and the paperwork event are different** (product is loaded or sprayed on one day and billed days later). Every ag suite is built around those two facts. Below is the full chain, why each step exists, and what it locks or posts. Items from Agvance's public help center are cited; general-practice statements are marked "(practice)", and conclusions we drew ourselves are marked "(inferred)". AgWorks' own versions of these steps are mostly unpublished, so none of this is claimed for AgOS except where marked.

### Step 1: Quote or plan

A quote or field plan is a proposal: products, rates, prices. **It locks nothing and posts nothing.** In Agvance the "order" family includes Plans (nutrient recommendations, field plans, quick sales orders) and Sales Orders, which import into a delivery ticket, booking or field plan and are then marked processed so they cannot be imported twice ([SKY Customer orders](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/orders)). The one-time-import rule is a small but important guard against double-selling, and CRX's equivalent is its quote status trigger and idempotency keys.

### Step 2: Booking (price lock and quantity commitment)

A booking is a commitment to buy a quantity at a price. It exists because growers want next year's price today and retailers want next year's demand today. In Agvance a booking can be unpaid (locks price and quantity, no money), prepaid (money received), a down payment (part paid now, price locked), or generic prepay (a lump sum applied automatically later) ([Invoicing Bookings](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/invoicing-bookings)). Bookings carry start and expiry dates, but an expired booking still warns rather than blocks, and "Quant Left" and "Dollars Left" show what remains ([Agvance Booking](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/regular-booking)). **What it locks:** the price (at invoicing, the booked price wins unless someone ticks "Retain Invoice Price"). **What it posts:** nothing for an unpaid booking. (Inferred: an unpaid, undelivered booking is a commitment, not a liability.)

### Step 3: Prepay (customer money before delivery)

Growers prepay mainly in the fourth quarter for tax reasons; tax advisers say a prepayment only counts as a purchase if the invoice names a definite quantity, quality and price with no refund or substitution right, while a bare credit on account is only a deposit ([UNL CropWatch](https://cropwatch.unl.edu/2018/prepaid-expenses); [CLA](https://claconnect.com/en/resources/blogs/prepaid-farm-expenses-reminder-a)). That makes **the wording of the prepay receipt a business-critical output**, not a cosmetic one. On the retailer's books, large ag companies treat prepayments as a liability (a "contract liability") until product ships and control passes ([FMC 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/37785/000003778522000075/fmc-20220630.htm); [Andersons 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/821026/000082102626000010/ande-20251231.htm)). Agvance's rule is blunt: any prepay money must sit on a booking, either product-specific or a generic prepay of a defined type ([Prepaying Products on Blend Ticket](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/prepaying-products-on-blend-ticket)). The customer record shows separate Regular, Prepay and unapplied-cash balances ([Agvance Booking](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/regular-booking)), and at month end a "Move A/R Prepay" step shifts prepay to its own general-ledger account and reverses at close ([month-end process](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/full-accounting-end-of-month-process)). **What it locks:** the money to a purpose. **What it posts:** a liability, not revenue. Agvance does not publish the exact debit and credit accounts, so treat "liability" as our reading plus the large-company disclosures.

Leftover prepay at season end has three exits in Agvance: leave it for next season, move it to the regular balance, or refund by check. The refund is deliberately a chain of three documents (a transfer invoice, an adjustment payment, and a check to a designated refund vendor), each voidable, so there is never a direct edit of a balance ([Transfer Customer Prepay](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/transfer-customer-prepay)). Agvance also adds an interest option on prepay and notes that invoice discounts do not apply to prepaid lines, so any prepay discount must be given when the booking is entered ([Posting Invoices](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/posting-invoices)).

### Step 4: Order or ticket (plan the physical event)

Agvance separates a **delivery ticket** (product movement) from an **invoice** (money). The ticket header carries customer and split, field, ordered and loaded dates, ship-from location, a Loaded flag, and an **On Hold flag that blocks billing**; lines carry gross, tare, net, load amount, optional lot number, crop code and container ID ([Delivery Tickets](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/add-delivery-ticket)). **While unloaded, a ticket has no inventory effect and cannot be invoiced.** A Pending Usage report projects on-hand stock from unloaded tickets, which is how the warehouse sees what is coming without having moved anything.

### Step 5: Blend and load-out

Blend tickets add a recipe layer (products by weight, priced by product or by analysis) and can import a completed file from an automated blender, which marks the ticket Loaded and relieves inventory ([Import Automated Blender Done Files](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/managing-blend-tickets-import-automated-blender-done-files), digest-only). **Marking Loaded is the event that moves inventory.** Editing a loaded ticket is allowed but leaves an "edited after loaded" trail rather than being blocked; a loaded ticket saved with a backorder creates a new unloaded ticket for the remainder ([Delivery Tickets](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/add-delivery-ticket)). A separate "Blend to Stock" function formulates a blend into finished-goods inventory instead of billing it, lowering each raw material and updating the finished product's cost ([Blend to Stock](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/add-blend-tickets-blend-to-stock)).

### Step 6: Delivery or application (the compliance moment)

For restricted-use pesticides the sale is regulated, so Agvance checks the customer's unexpired federal or state permit at invoice, delivery ticket and blend ticket, with three settings: don't check, warn only, or disallow; a permit's expiry must be on or after the transaction date; custom-applied transactions are exempt; and the selected permit prints on the document ([Regulation of Restricted Product](https://agvancehelpcenter.knowledgeowl.com/home/hub-products-regulation-of-restricted-product)). Credit is checked at the same moment: warn, stop sale, or stop with password, and loaded-but-unbilled tickets count against the available credit ([Credit Checking](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/credit-checking)). Field work is a "work order" saved as a delivery ticket, dispatched to an applicator app that records the job and e-signature ([Work Orders](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/customer-work-orders); [Apply app](https://agvance.net/post/apply-app-from-agvance-is-now-available-on-phones)). State rules differ and are not uniform; for example, one Midwestern state requires a license check before a restricted-use sale and records by pick-up date, kept five years ([MDA](https://mda.state.mn.us/restricted-use-pesticide-distribution-guidelines)).

### Step 7: Invoice (where everything converges)

The invoice is where pricing, prepay relief and accounting posting meet. A loaded ticket **must** be imported into an invoice (this prevents adjusting inventory twice); the invoice then affects customer A/R and revenue, and inventory only if the quantity changed at invoicing ([Delivery Tickets](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/add-delivery-ticket)). The same invoice function handles imported tickets and direct entry, numbers invoices per retail location, and shows a status per line: blank, Paid, Book, Dwn (down payment) or Book/Paid ([Invoicing Bookings](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/invoicing-bookings)). Matching a line to a booking follows a fixed precedence: product-specific by field, product-specific, classification-generic, then all-products generic ([Bookings Hierarchy](https://agvancehelpcenter.knowledgeowl.com/home/bookings-hierarchy)). **Splits** bill one job to several parties by percentage per inventory department, using up to three named split arrangements assigned to farms and fields (a landlord-tenant example splits most items 50/50 but chemicals differently and custom application fully to the tenant); the invoice prints a "shared with" area ([Bill Split Arrangements](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/bill-split-arrangements)). **What it posts:** revenue and A/R at invoice; invoicing credits each department's sales account ([Inventory Posting and Income Statement](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/inventory-posting-and-the-income-statement)).

### Step 8: Payment and credits

Payments apply to invoices with proration of early-pay discounts on partial payments ([A/R FAQs](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/ar-faqs)). Returns and billing errors are **credit invoices**: enter positive quantities, the system negates them and updates inventory and balance. A separate "Reconcile Credit Invoices" step applies credits and unapplied cash oldest-to-newest to open invoices by posting a zero-dollar payment, and it must be run before finance charges and statements ([Credit Invoices](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/credit-invoices)). The ordering matters: a customer should not be charged interest on a balance a credit already covers.

### Step 9: Void or reverse, statements, finance charges, close

Agvance has two distinct tools. **Void** works only in the current fiscal month; it removes the invoice from the customer account, inventory and general ledger, reopens the source tickets, and the invoice vanishes from the statement. **Reverse** works any time, even on paid invoices: the original stays in its month and a negating invoice lands in the current month, and both print on the statement. A location setting can make a reason mandatory, and COD invoices cannot be voided or reversed ([Void/Reverse Invoice](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/void-reverse-invoice)). Finance charges are computed on open invoices past a cutoff, with a whole-number rate, per-customer overrides (a special value excludes a customer), minimum charge and minimum balance, and review versus automatic modes; they create an **invoice of a special "Finance Charge" item**, so they flow through normal A/R and can be voided or reversed like any invoice ([Finance Charges](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/finance-charges)). Statements are a month-end run that cannot be regenerated once a new month begins, so they are archived. The month-end checklist is long and strict: an accounting checkup report, clearing accounts at zero, reconcile credits, finance charges, move prepay, post inventory, archive reports, back up, then close the fiscal month with an API lock ([month-end process](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/full-accounting-end-of-month-process)).

### Why the whole design hangs together

(Inferred.) Each document type has a status that answers one question: has it touched inventory, has it touched the customer's account, has it been closed? That is why ag suites feel rigid: rigidity is the control. The cost is training burden and workaround recipes. Agvance's own FAQ prescribes a negative booking plus a negative payment to void a booking payment when several payments are on it, and tells users that paid invoices are "not to be voided" ([A/R FAQs](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/ar-faqs)). CRX can take the controls without copying the workarounds.

## 2. Product detail: what each system does, and how sure we are

### Agvance (SSI): the best-documented reference

**Who and how big.** SSI is based in Shelbyville, Illinois, and reports 45 years in business, 200+ staff, about 750 retail customers and 25,000+ users across 3,800+ locations (company figures, [SSI](https://agvance.net/post/software-solutions-integrated-llc-celebrates-45-years)); it also claims that 53% of the largest retailers on a trade ranking are customers ([SSI](https://agvance.net/post/ssi-serves-over-450-million-of-the-900-million-farmable-acres-in-the-u-s)). These are vendor claims; no independent market-share figure was found, and the 53% is a share of the largest retailers, not of all retailers (inferred).

**Module structure.** One ERP with Accounting, Agronomy, Energy, Grain and Grower360 modules ([Why Agvance](https://agvance.net/why-agvance)). The older Windows client is menu-driven by module then function (paths like Accounting / A/R / Invoice, or Inventory / Delivery Ticket), with a shared "Hub" for setup such as bill-split arrangements and per-location tabs for preferences and posting. A newer cloud suite called SKY runs on AWS alongside the Windows client, with role-based web apps: Order, Dispatch (with a "Blend Shed"), Warehouse, Customer, Admin, Energy, Analytics, Ledger ([Why Agvance SKY](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/why-agvance-sky)). Windows is still required for some setup. (Inferred: the accounting core is a classic client/server system with web front ends on the same data.)

**Accounting.** A full general ledger. Two inventory-posting modes: *summarized* (inventory asset accounts move only when "Post Inventory to G/L" runs, usually monthly, valuing stock at a chosen cost basis with cost of sales as the offset) and *transactional* (every sale, purchase and adjustment posts as it happens) ([Posting Inventory](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/posting-inventory)). Inventory adjustments change the value used by the next post rather than hitting the ledger directly, so large adjustments distort a month unless corrected before posting. Vendor rebates are modeled as purchase add-on charges with a negative rate that accrue to an "anticipated rebate" account: debit cost of sales and credit the accrual on purchase, then debit cash and credit the accrual when the rebate arrives, with any difference to cost of sales ([Seed Discount Method 3](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/seed-discount-method-3)). Month-end tie-outs require customer balances to equal the A/R account, vendor balances to equal A/P, and the inventory statement to equal the inventory asset account; a nonzero "clearing" balance signals an incomplete process ([month-end process](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/full-accounting-end-of-month-process)).

**Inventory.** Multi-location with automatic inter-company transfers when a ticket ships from a different location; optional lot numbers with negative-lot controls (no checking, warn, or disallow); per-location switches to block negative on-hand; average, last and replacement costs; blends as formulas that relieve raw materials either at manufacture or at billing ([Location Preferences](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/location-preferences)). Products have an **inventory unit** (what is stocked and sold) and a separate **billing unit** (what the price is quoted in) joined by a divisor, for example pounds stocked and priced per ton ([Product Setup](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/product-setup-in-accounting)). Consignment is a product checkbox. In-transit and vendor returns were not found in public pages.

**Screens.** Invoices are grids of product lines with a Level column that opens a booking detail popup and a status column; setup lives in location tabs; dispatch has job grids, a map, colored priorities, vehicle positions and applicator assignment ([SKY Dispatch](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/sky-dispatch)); Grower360 shows balance cards for Regular, Prepay and Budget plus recent payments, invoices, applications and requested orders ([Grower360 Overview](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/g360-overview-page)). Screens are described from help text only; no video or screenshot review was possible.

**Praise and complaints.** Independent reviews are essentially absent (zero on one major review site; one older review called energy and accounting modules clunky, [SoftwareConnect](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/agvance-accounting/)). The pain points visible indirectly are the void workarounds, many import error codes, and a long manual month-end checklist (inferred from [A/R FAQs](https://helpcenter.agvance.net/home/ar-faqs)). Treat "strong integrity, rigid, heavy to learn" as an inference from documentation, not customer testimony.

### AgWorks AgOS: thin public evidence, plainly flagged

**What is solid.** AgWorks Software, LLC (Davenport, Iowa) is a division of The McGregor Company, which bought it in 2012; the product began in 1992 as real-time inventory for agronomy retail ([AgWorks about](https://www.agworks.net/about)). AgOS covers Operations (orders, inventory, blending, logistics), Crop Planning, Mapping, Scouting, Compliance, Grower Access and field apps, with partner tools HighQ (decision support) and Stratum (data cleaning) ([AgOS](https://www.agworks.net/agos); [brochure](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6272e33bbeab9b5aa538b1f9/t/63347787728aff7fc282694b/1664382856911/AgWorks_Marketing_Brochure.pdf)). Operations "unifies" crop plans, work orders, logistics, product sales, inventory adjustments and location transfers. The Compliance module tracks placarding, EPA/DOT history, tank inspections and automatically builds applicator logbooks from field events. **Grower Access shows a grower contract/booking information, pre-invoices, delivery tickets, work orders, farm maps and "split information."** The iPad app "AgOS In The Field" (first released February 2025) gives applicators and drivers work orders, batches, blend quantities, load lists, multi-bin orders and applied acres ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/agos-in-the-field/id6504048464)).

**What it suggests (inferred, not proven).** The work order looks like the hub: planning, mapping and scouting create it, blend/scale tools fulfil it, the mobile app closes it, and the compliance logbook falls out of it. The words "pre-invoice" and "split information" imply a draft billing document and split billing exist, and a plausible chain is booking, work order, load list, delivery ticket, pre-invoice, accounting invoice. This is the single most useful idea to test with AgWorks, because it differs from Agvance (where the delivery ticket is the hub) and from CRX (separate records).

**What is not public.** What each step creates, locks or carries forward; how bookings draw down; how prepay is accounted for; whether AgOS has its own general ledger. One directory listing says built-in accounting, while another lists an outside ag ERP (iRely i21) as the integration, and a 2015 press item says only that integration points exist to "most popular ag retail accounting systems" ([AgNewsWire](https://agnewswire.com/?p=3087); [SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/AgOS-Operations/)). Our best guess (weak) is that AgOS runs operations and hands finished invoices to a separate accounting system. No independent customer reviews exist. Pricing, headcount and the "Mix Plant Manager" name come only from third-party listings and should not be relied on. Several useful articles were unreachable (two trade-press features returned access errors) and are worth reading by hand.

### Cultura, AgVantage and others

Cultura is a roll-up of agri-food software businesses under Constellation Software (Volaris) that keeps acquiring; its ag retail relevant ERP is Greenstone AGRIS, with an AgroGuide agronomy module ([AgGateway: Cultura](https://aggateway.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/APM/pages/69895092/Cultura+Technologies+LLC)). Nothing here is applied to the live database without Mason's approval in the conversation, and destructive migrations need explicit approval.

## 5. Where CRX already leads, and what not to copy

CRX's sent-quote price lock with a per-change log, its database-enforced idempotency keys, its draw-down tracking, its per-field landlord split invoicing, its first-class applicator and traceability modules and its admin-only cost visibility are stronger or more modern than the Agvance help center describes for the same functions (compared from docs, not from running Agvance). Do not copy Agvance's void workarounds, the booking-payment recipes that need negative entries, or its month-end checklist length; automate the checks instead. Do not copy the general ledger, per the existing decision. CRX's "ship now, price later" and offline limitations are known and not evidence of a defect in the model.

Two items deserve a deliberate Mason look: the "internal-only, no grower portal" direction is sensible for now, but Agvance's Grower360 shows that growers expect to see prepay, unpaid invoices and applications, so revisit it after the switch-over; and the shelved prepay earmark engine was shelved for the right reason (double-spend), so any redesign must be a database rule, not a screen rule.

## 6. Questions to ask AgWorks

Plain-English, ready to send. Each one fills a gap named above.

1. **Prepay accounting.** When a grower prepays, what is recorded and where: a liability, a customer deposit, or a credit on the customer account? Is it tied to a booking, a product, or just the customer? What are the exact ledger entries when it is received and when it is applied to an invoice?
2. **Booking draw-down.** How does a booking get used up by deliveries? Does it track quantity left and dollars left, can an invoice draw from more than one booking, and what happens when a delivery exceeds the booked quantity or the booking expires?
3. **Price lock.** Where does the locked price live, and what stops a salesperson from overriding it at invoicing? Is the override logged?
4. **Split billing.** What does "split information" mean in Grower Access: landlord/tenant percentages, split by product category, or both? Are splits fixed per field, per order, or per delivery, and what happens when a split changes after part of an order is delivered?
5. **Work-order hub.** Is the work order the central record that bookings, blends, deliveries and compliance logbooks attach to? What does it create, lock, and carry forward at each stage?
6. **Pre-invoice.** What is a pre-invoice exactly? Who can edit it, when does it become a real invoice, and does the accounting system receive it or a final invoice?
7. **Accounting boundary.** Does AgOS contain its own general ledger, accounts receivable and accounts payable, or does it hand finished transactions to a separate accounting system? Which systems have you integrated with, and what exactly crosses the boundary (invoices, payments, inventory, adjustments)?
8. **Void versus reverse.** Can a posted or paid invoice be voided, or only reversed or credited? Does closing a period change what is allowed? What happens to the delivery, the booking and the prepay when an invoice is reversed?
9. **Inventory events.** At what exact moment does inventory decrease: when the work order is created, the blend is loaded, the delivery is confirmed, or the invoice is posted? What is the status model for that?
10. **Costing.** How is product cost determined (average, last, lot) and how does cost of goods sold post? Are rebates and freight included in cost?
11. **Rebates and programs.** Does AgOS accrue manufacturer rebates and early-order programs, and does it reconcile them to vendor statements?
12. **Compliance at sale.** How does AgOS handle restricted-use sales: license and permit checks at sale, blocking versus warning, and state reporting formats? Which states are supported?
13. **Credit control.** Is there a credit limit or hold, does it block a sale, and do loaded-but-unbilled tickets count against it?
14. **Finance charges and statements.** How are finance charges calculated and posted, can they be reviewed before posting, and are statements archived as issued?
15. **Implementation and data.** Is there a documented API or export, what does a typical migration from a legacy system look like, and can we see an anonymised sample of the screens for work orders, bookings and invoices?
