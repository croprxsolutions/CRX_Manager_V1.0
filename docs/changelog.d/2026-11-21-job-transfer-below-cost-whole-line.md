## 2026-11-21 — Create Invoice on a spray job writes each chemical's real quantity

- New migration `20270225160000_job_transfer_below_cost_whole_line.sql` (not applied live; record in `docs/reference/migrations.d/`).
- Before: Create Invoice wrote every chemical line as quantity 1 with the whole line total as its price, and the below-cost check compared that total with ONE stock unit's cost. A 3 fl oz line ($1.17) of a product stocked by the gallon, half a gallon, or any customer-supplied $0 line stopped Create Invoice, while 10 gallons sold under cost passed. The "1" also reached the restricted-use sales register and the sales reports.
- Now (Mason, 2026-11-22, "Redesign"): each line carries its real quantity in the product's sold unit with a price per sold unit, the way field-application invoices already do (3 fl oz = 0.0234 Gal at $49.92 = $1.17). The ordinary below-cost check judges it exactly, so no exemption is needed and the invoice screen can re-save it. A line genuinely below cost stops Create Invoice naming the product (never the cost); nothing is invoiced.
- A grower's own product is a $0 information line with no catalog link (name, EPA number, rate and amount still print; never counted as our sale). A unit that can't be converted to the sold unit (Ea on a gallon product, a per-acre unit), a product with no cost, and rounding that would move a line by more than 50 cents or bill it at $0 each stop Create Invoice and name the product (Mason, 2026-11-22). Two owners split the quantity by acre share at one price.
- The header, shares and commissions read a line's booked cost as cost x quantity; every invoice bills the sum of its lines, and its owner shares split those lines to the cent even when a field's split totals 99.99% (Luna round 2). A field's per-acre price is no longer billed by Create Invoice (Mason, 2026-11-22: "we never bill flat per acre"), so those fields get the normal per-acre application fee, and a multi-owner job on one is no longer refused. A deleted job, and a job whose deleted blend ticket still has a live invoice, are refused (Sol round 1). The below-cost check itself, orders, quotes, invoices, deliveries and field-invoice saves are unchanged. Old invoices keep their quantity-1 lines.
- Overlap: Taha's open issue #2061 touches the same function (this closes its live-invoice case); no open PR re-emits it.
- Live 2026-11-21: 0 completed-and-unbilled jobs. Proof: `node scripts/smoke/prove-job-transfer-below-cost-real-schema.mjs`. Not yet verified on staging.
- Sol round 3: lines book the job's own saved cost (commissions unchanged in basis); a one-owner job's spraying fee covers every treated acre (it covered only fields with billing defaults); a rate written in words is refused as a quantity unit; the owners' booked cost is held within 50 cents of the line. The invoice and statement PDFs print a field-application price per the line's own unit ("$10.00 Qt", not "per GL"). Emailed PDFs pick this up when the send-email function is next deployed.
- Sol round 4: lines book today's catalog cost per sold unit again (a job's saved cost can be per a different unit than its quantity, or $0); a two-owner job whose first line is the grower's own product no longer fails; the job screen's transfer wording now says each owner gets their own invoice and that a field's per-acre price is not billed.
- Sol round 5: a field with no billing split stops Create Invoice by name instead of falling back to a payer (Mason, 2026-11-22: every field has a split); grams, kilograms, milligrams, millilitres and litres convert exactly; a hidden character can no longer disguise a per-acre rate as a quantity unit.
- Taha's review: a job price that comes to a fraction of a cent per sold unit (40 Gal at $41.50 on a product sold by the Qt = $10.375 per Qt) now gets its own message naming both units and the nearest working job price ($41.52), instead of the 4-decimal rounding advice.
- Codex App review: a job unit equal to the product's sold unit (mg on a product stocked by the MG) is used as-is again; the metric conversion had refused it.
- Sol round 6: a job whose fields are all billed to one owner is now invoiced to that owner (header, balance owed, restricted-use buyer, spraying fee, commissions), even when the job's customer is someone else (Mason, 2026-11-22: each owner is billed for their own fields' acres). The job's fields and billing splits are locked while Create Invoice runs, so a Field Setup save at the same moment can't remove a split mid-way, and the old fallback that billed a field without a split to its own customer is gone. The round-5 metric conversion is withdrawn: job completion takes a metric quantity out of stock unconverted, so converting it on the invoice would disagree with stock. A metric unit is used only when it is the product's sold unit; any other stops Create Invoice and names the product.
- Sol round 8 (Mason, 2026-11-22): Create Invoice bills the billing split saved on the job, the one the job screen shows (a field with none saved falls back to its Field Setup split, as the screen does); the screen's confirmation names the customer a one-owner invoice goes to. Once a job is invoiced its chemicals, fields and billing split can't be changed (JOB_INVOICED_LOCKED) until its invoice is voided; a completed job stays editable, and a change made while Create Invoice runs waits for it and is then refused. The round-7 refusal of a grower-supplied line that took company stock is withdrawn: it refused a valid job using company stock and the grower's own of one product. Editing a completed job's chemicals still does not adjust stock already taken (pre-existing; follow-up).
- #2283 (Mason, 2026-11-22: "Refuse now, build later"): `save_job` refuses any change to a completed job's chemical lines (product, quantity, unit, grower-supplied) with JOB_COMPLETED_CHEMICALS_LOCKED, so the stock completion took can no longer drift from the job. The job screen already hid Save on completed jobs, so nothing changes for office staff; other changes to a completed job (fields, notes) still save. Editing a completed job's chemicals with stock following is a separate follow-up.
