What it does:
- New one-row `product_rate_lock` (starts unlocked) and append-only `product_rate_events`. A private `product_rate_correction_passes` table (row security on, no API-role access) holds the correction's one-transaction pass, so no session can forge it. Every signed-in user can read both; nobody writes them directly, and updates, deletes and truncates are refused.
- While locked, a trigger on `products` refuses any change to `rate_per_acre`, `rate_unit` or `suggested_rate` (`PRODUCT_RATES_LOCKED`), and refuses a new product saved with rates. Open or locked, every writer is held to the rate writers' rules: at most 6 decimals, and a unit known for the product's form with a conversion factor above zero, the same in every `unit_conversions` row spelled alike but for case, since pricing takes any one of them (`PRODUCT_RATE_INVALID`, `PRODUCT_RATE_UNIT_INVALID`); an unchanged legacy unit is kept only while `unit_conversions` lists it with such a factor, except on seed and each products. Read on live 2026-11-04: every product with a rate passes. Saves that leave the rates unchanged still work while locked, including stray spaces in the suggested rate and the seed/each rate unit that a new product takes from its inventory unit. A spaces-only change to the rate unit counts as a change, because per-acre pricing reads the unit exactly, and switching a product's form cannot move its rate unit while locked. On a rated product, a stock-unit (`inventory_unit`, else `unit_size`) or form change moves the per-acre price, so it counts as a rate change: refused while locked, and held to the unit rule while open. A kept seed or each rate unit is refused if `unit_conversions` lists it with a zero or missing factor. Pricing converts every rate through `unit_conversions`, so while locked a second trigger refuses any insert, update, delete or truncate of that table (`PRODUCT_RATES_LOCKED`); unlock with a reason first. No app screen writes it, and only admins may.
- The price workbook can no longer stage rate changes: a change-set row whose changes name a rate column is refused (`PRODUCT_RATES_READ_ONLY_IN_PRICE_WORKBOOK`). The migration refuses to install, changing nothing, while a preview staged earlier still names a rate (live had no staged rows at all when read).
- The three per-acre tier prices must be whole cents, 0 or more, and finite (CHECK constraints). Read on live before writing: 0 of 604 products break this.
- New `set_product_rates(rows, performed_by, idempotency_key)`: admin only, actor-bound, idempotent bulk rate writer for the CSV import (WP-PM-10) and setup workbook (WP-PM-11a-2). Refused while locked. Each row carries the expected current rates plus the pricing version, pack size, stock unit and form its per-acre preview was computed from, and the previewed tier 1–3 per-acre prices, which every written row must save exactly (`PRODUCT_RATE_PREVIEW_CHANGED` otherwise); stale rows, invalid rates (more than 6 decimals or 12 digits), unknown units and a rate with no unit (except seed and each products, whose unit follows the stock unit) are refused by name, all or nothing.
- New `set_product_rate_lock(locked, reason, performed_by, idempotency_key)`: admin only, reason required (at most 500 characters), logged.
- New `correct_product_rates(product, rate, unit, suggested, expected_pricing_version, expected_prices, reason, performed_by, idempotency_key)`: only while locked (`PRODUCT_RATES_NOT_LOCKED` otherwise), an admin corrects one product with a reason; checks the pricing version, logs before and after, and reprices per acre. It refuses and saves nothing (`PRODUCT_RATE_PREVIEW_CHANGED`) unless the saved tier 1–3 per-acre prices equal the previewed ones the admin confirmed, since a unit conversion changed after the preview moves prices without moving the pricing version.
- New `preview_product_rate_correction(product, rate, unit)`: admin only, read-only; returns the tier 1–3 per-acre prices before and after a proposed correction, computed the way the save will compute them. The correction window shows it before the save button appears.
